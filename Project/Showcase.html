Bachelor in Engineering Technology

MAJOR IN COMPUTER ENGINEERING TECHNOLOGY

WHY CHOOSE COMPUTER ENGINEERING TECHNOLOGY?

The world runs on technology, and we're the ones who build and maintain it. Choosing this path means diving into the hardware and software that power everyday life. It’s for creators who want to solve real-world problems, lead multi-disciplinary teams, and make a tangible impact on a global scale. Whether you are wiring logic circuits or writing code, this program sets you up to thrive in a fast-paced, ever-evolving industry.

WHAT WE DO

We bridge the gap between complex theory and hands-on reality. Every day, we design, analyze, and build systems to meet specific needs while navigating realistic constraints. From conducting experiments to identifying and solving complex engineering problems, our work is highly collaborative. We don't just build tech; we evaluate its impact on society and ensure everything we create is rooted in professional and ethical responsibility.

OUR GOAL

Our ultimate mission is to shape adaptable, hands-on innovators who are ready to lead. We aim to equip students with the modern tools and management principles needed to contribute directly to the tech industry — especially small and medium-scale enterprises. We want our graduates to step confidently into key professional positions, manage complex projects, and champion lifelong learning in whatever specialization they choose.

WHAT YOU WILL LEARN?

You will master the core fundamentals of math, science, and engineering, but you won't just be stuck in a textbook. You'll get your hands dirty with modern engineering tools and techniques, learning how to design systems and interpret complex data. Beyond the technical hardware and software skills, you will also learn how to communicate effectively, manage multidisciplinary projects, and stay aware of contemporary issues shaping the tech landscape.

Projects


Logic Gates Introductory Activity

S09 applied DeMorgan's Theorems to simplify a complex Boolean expression, reducing the required component count for a more efficient hardware build. The math was translated into a working prototype by mapping standard 74-series TTL ICs onto a breadboard using jumper wires. The final circuit was then verified by toggling input switches to match the physical LED output against a derived truth table.

Intro to KiCAD: Etching & Soldering Basic Electronics Circuit

S09 designed a 9V bridge circuit in KiCad and fabricated a custom PCB using a ferric chloride chemical etch. After drilling and soldering the components, the final board was tested to verify that the physical voltage readings across the 1K load resistor matched their theoretical calculations.

D.I.Y Power Supply Base

Custom power control utilizing a 4.5" x 10" base and a front panel. A transformer was mounted to the base and wired directly to a front-facing AC power socket, fuse holder, and 3-pin switch. All electrical connections between the transformer and the front panel components were manually soldered and fully insulated with heat shrink tubing to ensure safe and reliable power routing.
